# Incident disclosure 2026-11-10

## Summary

- On 10 November 2026 an attacker diverted **38,205.76 USDC** of Curve DAO fee revenue from the `CowSwapBurner` contract on Ethereum[[1]](#references), the contract that sells collected fees for crvUSD through CoW Protocol.
- **No user funds were affected.** The burner holds only admin fees that have already been collected for the DAO. Pools, lending markets and crvUSD were not involved.
- The burner approved CoW Protocol orders without checking that the order it had inspected was the order being executed. The attacker used this to place 138 orders that sold the burner's tokens and paid the proceeds to their own address[[3]](#references).
- What was taken was a backlog of small balances across 135 tokens. That day's regular conversion, 97,317.74 crvUSD, completed normally.
- The Emergency DAO disabled the burner at 13:59 UTC the same day. A replacement burner that closes the gap is in a DAO vote[[5]](#references).
- The funds have not been recovered.

## Background

Curve collects admin fees from its pools and markets into the `FeeCollector`[[2]](#references) and converts them to crvUSD once a week. Fees are moved to a burner contract on Monday, the burner sells them during a 24-hour exchange window that opens on Tuesday at 00:00 UTC, and the crvUSD is distributed on Wednesday.

Since June 2024 the burner on Ethereum has been the `CowSwapBurner`. For each fee token it holds, it offers a standing order on CoW Protocol: sell the whole balance for crvUSD, deliver the crvUSD to the `FeeCollector`, and accept no less than 500 crvUSD. To allow this, it grants CoW Protocol an unlimited allowance for each token.

The burner is a contract and cannot sign orders, so CoW Protocol asks it directly whether an order is valid. It does this through the ERC-1271 standard, calling `isValidSignature(orderHash, signature)` on the burner and proceeding only if the answer is yes.

Before the incident the burner had settled 6,420 orders and delivered about 20.5 million crvUSD.

## Details of vulnerability

`isValidSignature` receives two things: the hash of the order CoW Protocol is about to execute, and a block of signature data. For the burner, the signature data contains a full description of an order.

The burner checked that the described order was one of its own (crvUSD out, the `FeeCollector` as receiver, at least 500 crvUSD) and answered yes. It never checked that the described order was the one identified by the hash. The function's own documentation records this (excerpt):

```python
def isValidSignature(_hash: bytes32, signature: Bytes[1792]) -> bytes4:
    """
    @notice ERC1271 signature verifier method
    @param _hash Hash of signed object. Ignored here
    @param signature Signature for the object. (GPv2Order.Data, PayloadStruct) here
    """
```

In effect, the burner could be shown a legitimate order and would then approve a different one. Anyone could submit an order in the burner's name, selling any token it held for any other token and to any receiver, and attach the description of a genuine burner order as the signature data. The only requirement was that the exchange window was open.

This can be reproduced without moving funds. At block 26,380,819, just before the first forged order, the burner answers yes for randomly generated hashes when given the signature data used in the exploit.

The repository history shows that the missing comparison was drafted during development but never enabled. It was added in commented-out form in March 2024[[8]](#references) and deleted in May 2024[[9]](#references), before deployment.

## Details of exploit

The exchange window opened at 00:00 UTC on Tuesday 10 November 2026 and the burner's regular orders began to fill.

At 00:07 UTC the first forged order settled[[6]](#references). It sold the burner's YB balance for USDC and named the attacker as receiver. Attached to it was the burner's own genuine order for YB. The attacker then reused that same attachment for 137 further orders selling other tokens. 133 orders filled within 17 minutes and the remaining five over the following hours, the last at 11:10 UTC[[7]](#references).

| | Orders | Tokens | Proceeds | Paid to |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | :--- |
| Regular | 36 | 35 | 97,317.74 crvUSD | `FeeCollector` |
| Forged | 138 | 135 | 38,205.76 USDC | Attacker |

The forged orders were otherwise ordinary. They were priced close to market and filled by 45 different solver addresses competing as usual. Solvers executed them as written.

The two groups did not overlap: the forged orders sold only tokens that the regular orders did not. These were mostly balances too small to meet the burner's 500 crvUSD minimum, which had built up unsold over time. The median forged order paid 254 USDC and only two paid more than 500. For the tokens that had been sold before, the median time since the last sale was 161 days.

Every order settled for the burner since its deployment, 6,558 in total, was checked. These 138 are the only ones not paid to the `FeeCollector`.

### Movement of funds

Within four hours the attacker swapped 37,611.76 USDC to ETH, split it across four addresses, bridged it to Arbitrum and deposited 37,229.89 USDC into Hyperliquid[[10]](#references), where it was exchanged for 65.41 XMR1, a Monero-linked asset. As of 16 November that balance had not moved.

On 15 November the remaining 594 USDC was swapped to ETH, and 0.3 ETH was deposited into Tornado Cash[[11]](#references).

## Details of fix

**Immediate mitigation.** At 13:59 UTC on 10 November the Emergency DAO disabled fee collection and exchange on the Ethereum `FeeCollector`[[4]](#references). The burner only approves orders while exchange is enabled, so from that block it rejects every order, and no new fees can reach it. The same was done on Arbitrum and Base at 16:55 UTC as a precaution[[12]](#references). No forged orders were found on Arbitrum or Gnosis.

Fee conversion on Ethereum is paused in the meantime. Fees continue to accrue and are not lost.

**Replacement.** DAO vote 1524[[5]](#references), which closes on 20 November 2026, replaces the `CowSwapBurner` with a new burner that sells fees through a descending-price auction. When it accepts a CoW Protocol order, it requires the hash it is given to match the order it has checked.

## Timeline of events

All times are UTC.

- **12 June 2024**: `CowSwapBurner` deployed on Ethereum.
- **25 October 2026**: Attacker's address funded.
- **10 November 2026, 00:00**: Exchange window opens.
- **00:07**: First forged order settles.
- **00:24**: 133 forged orders have settled.
- **03:53**: Proceeds have reached Hyperliquid.
- **11:10**: Last forged order settles.
- **~12:40**: An external report describing the vulnerability and the ongoing exploitation is received.
- **13:39**: Emergency DAO transaction to disable collection and exchange is proposed.
- **13:59**: Transaction executed. The burner can no longer approve orders.
- **16:55**: Equivalent transactions executed on Arbitrum and Base.
- **13 November 2026**: DAO vote 1524 opened.

## Third party disclosure

The `CowSwapBurner` code is public and has been reused outside Curve. Operators of known deployments derived from it were notified before this disclosure was published.
